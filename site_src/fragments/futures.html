    How the season is simulated

    • Team strength combines opponent-adjusted passing, rushing, special teams and point-margin ratings. The weights were learned from what predicted later games in 2010–2021.
    • This season's numbers take over from last season's as plays accumulate, at a pace learned separately for passing, rushing, special teams and scoring margin.
    • When a different starting quarterback is expected, the team's strength shifts by half the gap between the two quarterbacks' track records.
    • Each simulated season draws every team's strength once, with 3.5 points of uncertainty, then plays out every remaining game. Played games are locked in.
    • Ties in the standings use head-to-head, division record, conference record, then point differential. The official procedure has more steps, so very close races are approximate.
    • Playoffs use 7 seeds per conference, a bye for the No. 1 seed, reseeding, home field and a neutral Super Bowl.

    Read these numbers with care

    • Against historical bookmaker prices the model is roughly as accurate as the market only in mid-season. A big gap between this page and a bookmaker is more often model error than value.
    • Injuries other than quarterback changes are not modelled yet, and news after the latest games is not known until the next refresh.
    • Simulation noise is about ±0.4 percentage points at 50%, so small differences mean nothing.
